# HSM Strategic — SEO Growth Plan
*Content calendar, backlink targets, and account setup — companion to the technical SEO fixes already shipped*

## What's already done vs. what's left

The technical foundation is live once the pushed commits deploy: canonical tags, Open Graph/Twitter cards, JSON-LD schema, robots.txt, sitemap.xml, favicons, and a compressed logo. That work makes the site *crawlable and legible* to search engines. It does not, by itself, make Google want to rank it. What actually moves rankings from here is content depth and backlinks, both of which take sustained effort rather than a one-time fix. This plan breaks that remaining work into three tracks: a content calendar, a backlink/directory list, and the two account setups only you can do.

## Track 2: Where to get backlinks

Content and technical SEO get you found; other sites linking to you are what convince Google you're worth ranking. For a firm at this stage, three targets are worth the time:

**[Axial](https://www.axial.net/)** is the actual marketplace where boutique banks, independent sponsors, and lower middle-market funds — your stated audience — go to source deals and service providers. It supports a firm profile, and profiles there both drive direct inquiries and count as a relevant, authoritative backlink. Worth setting up regardless of the SEO angle, since it's literally where your buyers already are.

**[Crunchbase](https://support.crunchbase.com/hc/en-us/articles/115011823988-How-do-I-create-a-Crunchbase-profile)** is a free company profile that's low effort and shows up in its own search results plus company-lookup tools your prospects might use to vet you before a call. Five minutes, permanent backlink.

**[ACG / ACG Insights](https://www.acg.org/) (formerly Middle Market Growth)** is the trade association and publication for exactly your buyer base — PE firms, independent sponsors, corporate development teams in the middle market. Joining a local ACG chapter (there's one in most major cities) gets you into a community where guest-content and contributor relationships are realistic, and a byline or mention on middlemarketgrowth.org would be a genuinely high-authority backlink in your specific niche — far more valuable than a generic directory link.

Skip the generic B2B directories (Clutch, G2, and similar) — they're built for software and marketing agencies, not deal-research firms, and a mismatched listing does nothing for you. LinkedIn stays your primary distribution channel: it doesn't pass SEO link equity directly, but posting each new article there is what gets it in front of the people who might actually link to it.

This is what tells you whether any of this is actually working — impressions, clicks, average ranking position, per query.

**Google Analytics (GA4).** Create a property at analytics.google.com, and send me the measurement ID (looks like G-XXXXXXX) — I'll wire the tracking snippet into all seven pages in one pass. Right now there's no analytics installed anywhere on the site, so there's no way to see traffic at all until this is done.

Both take about five minutes each and neither requires anything I can do on your behalf, since they need your Google account.

## Suggested order

Push the pending commits first so the technical fixes are actually live, then do Search Console and GA4 the same day so you start collecting baseline data immediately. Set up Axial and Crunchbase profiles that week — they're one-time, low-effort tasks. Then start the content calendar at whatever pace is sustainable; even one article every two weeks compounds meaningfully over six months, and a rushed, inconsistent schedule is worse than a slower, reliable one.
